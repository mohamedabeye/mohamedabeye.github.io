About Me

I work where statistics, computing, and public finance meet. At the Inland Revenue Department of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Development, I design and maintain the national tax databases and the official statistics that support revenue management and evidence-based policy decisions.

My research asks how governments can forecast revenue reliably when historical series are short, incomplete and fast-changing. I compare simple and modern models out of sample, use Bayesian methods to show forecast uncertainty, and keep every analysis reproducible.

Alongside public-sector leadership, I lecture in programming and data structures at the University of Hargeisa and founded Damaana, a data and software consultancy.

Research & Publications

Preprint · 2026 DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24464265

Forecasting Customs Revenue in a Data-Scarce Economy: Revisiting a Linear Trend Model for Kalabaydh, Somaliland

Revisits the linear trend model from my 2015 MSc thesis and compares it with four alternatives on 14 years of customs revenue (1999–2012): a log-linear trend, Holt's exponential smoothing on the log scale, a random walk with drift on the log scale, and a Bayesian log-linear model. The random walk with drift cut out-of-sample forecast error (MAPE) from 41.1% to 14.6%. Data and code are openly available.
